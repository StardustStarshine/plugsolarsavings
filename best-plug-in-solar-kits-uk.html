Best Plug-in Solar Kits UK: What Should You Look For?

Comparing plug-in solar kits is about more than choosing the biggest panel rating or the lowest advertised price. This guide explains which specifications, features and ownership details deserve attention before you buy.

Last checked: 16 September 2026 · Great Britain rules can differ from Northern Ireland

There is no single best plug-in solar kit in the UK for every household. The right choice depends on your available space, mounting location, electricity use, budget, monitoring preferences and the exact product requirements that apply where you live.

When comparing products, look beyond phrases such as “800W”, “balcony solar” or “plug-and-play”. A useful plug-in solar kit comparison should consider the complete system: panels, inverter, mounting, cables, monitoring, warranties, total price and suitability for your property.

What should you compare when choosing a plug-in solar kit?

Retailers may present kits in very different ways. One may emphasise total panel wattage, another may focus on its app, while another may advertise a low starting price. Those details can all matter, but they describe different parts of the buying decision.

The sections below provide a practical framework for comparing like with like without assuming that one brand or product is automatically best.

1. Check compliance before comparing features

Compliance should come before deciding which kit has the most attractive app, mounting system or price. In Great Britain, the current plug-in solar route applies to qualifying products that meet the relevant requirements. The exact model and its documentation matter; an advert alone is not proof of compliance.

For the current framework, check the product’s technical information, required markings, testing and registration information, and the manufacturer’s instructions. The Plug-in Solar Kits UK guide covers the practical checks in more detail, while UK Rules & Safety provides a wider reference point.

If you are in Northern Ireland, do not assume that the Great Britain route applies automatically. Check the rules and connection requirements relevant to your location.

2. Compare the inverter, not just the headline panel wattage

The inverter is one of the most important parts of a plug-in solar kit because it converts the panels’ DC electricity into AC electricity for the intended connection.

Panel Wp

Watts peak (Wp) describe the rated DC output of the solar panels under defined test conditions. A kit may contain one or more panels whose combined Wp is stated by the seller.

AC output

The inverter has a declared AC output. This is not the same measurement as the panels’ combined Wp rating.

VA / apparent power

Apparent power, measured in volt-amperes (VA), is another inverter specification. It should be checked alongside the declared AC output and current.

Exact model

Ask for the inverter manufacturer, model number, technical datasheet and the ratings for the complete product being sold.

For the current Great Britain plug-in solar specification, the inverter’s maximum apparent power must not exceed 800 VA, and the relevant route refers to a maximum rated AC output of 800 watts and a maximum current to the installation of 3.5 A. These figures should be checked against the actual product documentation.

A kit with panels rated above the inverter’s AC output is not automatically unusual or unsuitable: panel DC capacity and inverter AC capability are different things. The important point is to understand the relationship rather than treating every wattage figure as interchangeable.

3. Compare panel capacity and physical size

When comparing a solar panel kit in the UK, look at both the total rated capacity and the physical arrangement needed to use it.

  • Total Wp: Check the combined rated panel capacity and how it is calculated.
  • Number of panels: More panels may affect space, mounting, handling and appearance.
  • Dimensions: Check the actual length, width, thickness and weight rather than relying on a product photograph.
  • Available space: Consider whether the proposed location can accommodate the complete kit safely and practically.
  • Orientation and shade: The position of the panels can affect generation, so a larger headline rating may not translate into a proportionally better result in every location.

More panel capacity is not automatically better for every home. A kit needs to fit the available space and the way the property can use the electricity it generates.

For a broader explanation of panel types and related equipment, see Panels & Components.

4. Compare mounting options

Mounting is a major part of the product comparison because it affects where the panels can be positioned, how much space they need and whether the proposed location is suitable.

Balcony mounting

Check the complete balcony mounting design, dimensions, weight, fixing approach and any restrictions affecting the building. A product described as “balcony solar” is not automatically suitable for every balcony.

Ground mounting

Compare the footprint, stability requirements, access, exposure to wind and whether the proposed outdoor area is appropriate for the equipment.

Wall or other mounting

Check the intended surface, structural suitability, mounting hardware included and any property permissions or restrictions that may apply.

For flats, rented properties, leasehold homes, listed buildings or properties with particular cladding or balcony arrangements, permissions and property-specific requirements may be just as important as the mounting price. This guide is a buying comparison, not an installation instruction: follow the manufacturer’s documentation and obtain appropriate professional advice where needed.

5. Compare monitoring and app features

Monitoring can help you understand how a kit is performing, but apps differ considerably. Before paying extra for monitoring, check what the system actually provides.

  • Live or near-real-time generation information.
  • Historical generation data by day, week or month.
  • Simple production graphs and alerts.
  • Information about inverter status or faults.
  • Whether the app requires Wi-Fi, a separate gateway or another connection.
  • Whether access is included permanently or depends on a subscription or service.

Monitoring can make it easier to see when solar is producing and may help a household understand when to use electricity during daylight hours. However, an app does not automatically increase savings; the financial benefit still depends on generation, electricity use and the amount of solar power that can be used rather than imported.

6. Compare warranties and after-sales support

Do not treat “the kit warranty” as one single promise without checking the details. Different components may have different warranty terms, and the company selling the kit may not be the manufacturer of every part.

Compare:

  • Panel warranty: What product coverage is provided for the panels?
  • Inverter warranty: How is the inverter covered, and are there registration requirements?
  • Performance warranty: Is a separate performance warranty offered for the panels, and what does it actually cover?
  • Claim process: Who handles a fault or warranty claim—the retailer, installer, distributor or manufacturer?
  • Conditions: Are there requirements relating to registration, installation, mounting, storage, use or maintenance?
  • Replacement and labour: Does the stated cover include only parts, or are other costs addressed?

Ask for the full warranty documents before buying rather than relying only on a headline number or a short product-page statement.

7. Compare the total price, not just the advertised price

A low headline price may not represent the final cost of getting a complete kit that suits your circumstances. Build a like-for-like comparison using the same list of components and services.

Include these in your price comparison:

  • Solar panels and inverter.
  • Mounting system and fixings.
  • Cables, connectors and the supplied plug or connection equipment.
  • Delivery or collection charges.
  • Monitoring hardware, if separate.
  • Any additional equipment the manufacturer says is required.
  • Potential property, professional or other project-specific costs.

Prices, stock and package contents change. If you compare live products, record the date, seller, exact model and what was included in the quoted price.

Two kits with similar panel wattage may have very different total prices because of mounting hardware, monitoring, warranty support, delivery or included accessories.

8. Consider how much solar you can actually use

The useful output of a kit is not only about how much electricity the panels can generate. It is also about how much of that electricity your household can use at the time it is produced.

  • Are people usually at home during daylight hours?
  • Do you run appliances, work equipment or other loads during the day?
  • Can your electricity use be shifted naturally towards sunny periods?
  • Will orientation or shade reduce production at the times you need it?
  • Have you allowed for seasonal variation, including shorter winter days and changing weather?

This is the difference between generation and self-consumption: electricity used directly in the home can reduce the amount bought from the grid, while unused generation may not deliver the same immediate value. The existing Plug Solar Savings calculator uses factors such as location, orientation, shade and household use to produce an illustrative estimate.

If you are considering a wider energy setup, you can also explore our Solar Batteries page. Battery storage is a separate consideration and should not be assumed to be included in a qualifying plug-in solar product.

Which type of kit suits your circumstances?

Rather than ranking brands, use your main priority to decide which characteristics deserve the most attention.

If your priority is lower upfront cost

Compare the complete delivered price and check exactly what is excluded. A cheaper kit may not be cheaper once mounting, delivery, monitoring or other required equipment is added.

If you have limited space

Prioritise accurate panel dimensions, total footprint, weight and a mounting approach that is appropriate for the available location. Compare usable placement rather than panel wattage alone.

If monitoring matters to you

Look for clear generation history, useful status information and transparent connectivity requirements. Confirm whether the app is included and whether any ongoing service cost applies.

If you want to understand your generation

Look for accessible data and documentation that explains the system’s ratings. Consider how orientation, shade, season and household demand will affect what you actually see.

If you may expand your wider home energy setup later

Check what the current kit includes and excludes, and do not assume that a plug-in solar product is designed for battery integration. Review separate battery or wider solar-system options before making future compatibility assumptions.

Questions to ask a seller before purchasing

You can copy this checklist when contacting a retailer or manufacturer:

  1. What is the exact inverter manufacturer and model?
  2. What is the inverter’s declared AC output?
  3. What is its maximum apparent-power rating in VA?
  4. What is the maximum current to the installation?
  5. What is the combined panel Wp rating?
  6. How many panels are included, and what are their dimensions and weights?
  7. Exactly what is included in the kit?
  8. What mounting system is included, and what locations is it designed for?
  9. What monitoring or app features are included?
  10. Does monitoring require additional hardware, Wi-Fi or a subscription?
  11. What are the separate panel, inverter and performance warranty terms?
  12. Who handles warranty claims and what conditions apply?
  13. What are the delivery charges and any other additional costs?
  14. Where can I verify the exact product’s compliance and relevant registration information?
  15. What property permissions or restrictions should I check before buying?

Great Britain and Northern Ireland

The current plug-in solar changes and product framework discussed in this guide relate to Great Britain. Northern Ireland has its own electricity-safety and network arrangements, so a product or guide aimed at Great Britain should not be treated as automatic confirmation that the same route applies in Northern Ireland.

Before purchasing or connecting a product, check the requirements that apply to your location and seek clarification from the relevant supplier, network operator or qualified professional where necessary.

Final buying checklist

Before choosing between plug-in solar kits, make sure you can answer these questions:

  • Is the exact product suitable for the rules and requirements where I live?
  • Have I compared the inverter’s AC and VA ratings as well as the panel Wp?
  • Do the panel dimensions and mounting system suit my available space?
  • Do I understand what the monitoring system actually provides?
  • Have I read the separate warranty terms for the main components?
  • Have I compared the complete delivered price?
  • Can my household use a meaningful proportion of the electricity when it is generated?
  • Have I checked property permissions and any location-specific restrictions?
