Solar Battery Storage UK

Store Your Solar. Save More.

Solar battery storage can help you use more of your own solar energy.

Solar panels generate most of their electricity during daylight hours. But many households use more electricity in the evening, when solar generation has fallen.

A home battery provides a way to store some of that surplus electricity and use it later instead of automatically buying as much electricity from the grid.

The important question is not simply whether a battery works. It is whether the potential savings justify the cost of the battery and installation for your particular home.

The simple idea

Generate solar electricity → store the surplus → use it later. The more useful solar energy you can consume yourself, the less electricity you may need to buy from the grid.

How solar battery storage works

A battery changes when you use your solar electricity. Instead of using or exporting surplus generation immediately, you can store some of it for later.

☀

1. Your panels generate electricity

Solar panels generate electricity when sunlight is available. Your home uses this electricity first.

↗

2. Surplus energy is stored

When your solar system produces more electricity than your home is using, compatible systems can send the surplus into a battery.

⚡

3. You use it later

Stored electricity can then be used when solar generation is lower, such as during the evening or at night.

How much money can a solar battery save?

There is no single saving that applies to every household. Your potential saving depends on how much surplus solar you have, when you use electricity and the tariff you pay.

More solar used at home

A battery can allow surplus daytime generation to be used later, potentially reducing electricity purchased from the grid.

Less wasted opportunity

Without storage, surplus solar electricity may be exported or otherwise unavailable to your home when you need it later.

Smart tariffs

Some batteries can also charge from the grid. With a suitable time-of-use tariff, this can create additional opportunities to shift electricity consumption.

Solar battery payback

A simple way to think about battery economics is to compare the installation cost with the estimated annual saving.

A simple example

Imagine a complete battery installation costs £4,000 and the system helps save an estimated £500 per year.

£4,000 ÷ £500 = approximately 8 years.

That is only an illustrative simple payback calculation. Actual savings can change because of electricity prices, export payments, tariffs, battery efficiency, household usage and changes in solar generation.

How much does a solar battery cost in the UK?

Battery prices vary considerably depending on the battery's usable capacity, chemistry, power output, inverter arrangement, installation requirements and manufacturer.

A complete home battery system can cost several thousand pounds. The cheapest battery is not necessarily the best-value option if it has lower usable capacity, poorer compatibility or a shorter warranty.

When comparing quotes, look at the complete installed cost rather than the battery price alone.

Don't compare capacity alone

Compare usable capacity, power output, round-trip efficiency, warranty, expected lifespan, installation cost, compatibility and the way the battery will actually be used.

UK solar battery VAT

VAT treatment can make a meaningful difference to the cost of eligible battery installations.

Check the current VAT rules before buying

Qualifying electrical storage batteries installed in residential accommodation can currently benefit from the zero rate of VAT in Great Britain, subject to the relevant conditions.

The rules also cover certain battery installations added to existing solar systems and qualifying standalone battery installations.

VAT rules can change, so check the current HMRC guidance and ask your installer how VAT applies to your specific installation.

Is a solar battery worth it?

Battery storage can make sense for one household and be poor value for another. These are some of the factors worth considering.

Your electricity usage

Homes that use significant amounts of electricity in the evening may have more opportunity to benefit from stored daytime solar.

Your solar surplus

If your solar system regularly generates more electricity than your home needs during the day, storage may allow more of it to be used later.

Your tariff

Electricity import prices and export payments affect the value of storing and using solar electricity rather than buying it later from the grid.

How big a solar battery do I need?

Bigger is not automatically better.

The right size depends on your electricity consumption, solar generation, how much surplus electricity your system produces, your tariff and when you typically use electricity.

A battery that is regularly cycled can potentially provide more value than a much larger battery that spends most of its time unused.

Think about your evening usage

Start by looking at how much electricity your household typically uses after your solar generation has fallen. This helps provide a better starting point for discussing battery capacity with an installer.

Can you add a battery to existing solar panels?

In many situations, yes. But the existing system needs to be checked for compatibility.

Check your existing equipment

Your inverter, wiring, electrical installation and battery system all need to work together correctly.

Some batteries use an integrated inverter, while other setups require additional equipment. Your installer should assess the existing solar installation before recommending a battery.

If you already have solar panels, ask installers specifically about retrofit battery options rather than assuming every battery will work with your existing equipment.

Solar batteries and plug-in solar

Battery storage becomes more complicated when you are dealing with plug-in solar rather than a conventional installed solar PV system.

Don't assume any battery will work

A plug-in solar kit and a home battery are not automatically compatible simply because both use solar electricity.

The electrical connection, inverter arrangement, battery controls and applicable UK requirements all need to be considered.

If your main interest is plug-in solar, start with the kit itself and check whether the manufacturer provides a supported battery or storage solution.

Battery storage vs exporting your solar electricity

A battery is not automatically the best financial choice. Sometimes exporting surplus electricity can be more attractive.

Using it yourself

Storing surplus electricity may allow you to use it later, potentially avoiding the need to buy electricity at a higher import price.

Exporting surplus

Some households may receive payment for exporting surplus electricity. The value depends on the export arrangement and tariff.

Compare both

The right decision depends on your import tariff, export payment, battery cost, expected usage and how much solar energy you can realistically shift.

Solar Battery FAQs

Quick answers to common questions about home battery storage.

What is a solar battery?

A solar battery stores electricity generated by solar panels so it can be used later, such as during the evening or at night. This can reduce the amount of electricity a household needs to buy from the grid.

How much does a solar battery cost in the UK?

Costs vary depending on capacity, installation requirements, battery technology and the wider system. A complete home battery installation can cost several thousand pounds, so it is important to compare quotes and calculate the potential savings.

Can a solar battery save money?

Yes, potentially. A battery can store surplus solar electricity and allow it to be used later instead of buying as much electricity from the grid. The actual saving depends on your electricity usage, solar generation, tariff, battery efficiency and system cost.

Is a solar battery worth it?

It can be, but not for every household. Compare the complete installation cost against your estimated annual saving and consider your solar generation, electricity usage and tariff.

How long does a solar battery last?

Many modern home battery systems are designed to last around 10 to 12 years, although actual lifespan depends on the battery technology, usage, operating conditions and manufacturer warranty.

How big a solar battery do I need?

The right size depends on your electricity usage, solar generation, surplus solar, tariff and when you use electricity. Bigger is not always better if the additional capacity is rarely used.

Can I add a battery to existing solar panels?

In many cases, yes. However, the existing inverter, electrical installation and battery system need to be checked for compatibility before installation.

Can a solar battery charge from the grid?

Some battery systems can charge from the grid as well as solar. This may be useful with certain time-of-use tariffs, depending on electricity prices, battery efficiency and the system's settings.

Can I use a battery with plug-in solar?

Not every battery can be connected directly to a plug-in solar system. Compatibility, the electrical design and applicable requirements need to be checked before combining the systems.

How do I calculate solar battery payback?

A simple estimate is the total battery and installation cost divided by estimated annual savings. For example, £4,000 divided by £500 per year gives an illustrative payback of around eight years. Actual results can vary.
