A simpler way to compare plug-in solar

Plug-in solar is a smaller-scale way of generating electricity for your home. Different kits can look very similar at first, but panel capacity, inverter output, mounting equipment, warranties and included components can vary significantly.

This guide brings those details together so you can understand what you're actually comparing before checking current prices or deciding which type of system could suit your property.

We focus on specifications and information that can be checked, rather than pretending to have personally tested products we haven't tested.

What is a plug-in solar kit?

A typical plug-in solar kit combines one or more solar panels with a microinverter, mounting equipment and the cables needed to connect the system. The panels generate DC electricity and the microinverter converts it into AC electricity for use in your home.

How does plug-in solar work?

The basic process is straightforward, although the equipment and installation requirements differ between products.

1

Solar panels receive sunlight during daylight hours.

2

3

4

5

Why do so many plug-in solar kits use 800W?

One of the easiest mistakes to make is confusing panel capacity with the amount of AC electricity the inverter can deliver.

Panel watts aren't the same as inverter watts

Many current GB plug-in solar systems use an inverter with an AC output around the 800W class. However, the solar panels connected to that inverter can have a higher combined DC capacity.

For example, a system could have 1,030Wp or 1,260Wp of solar panels while its microinverter remains in the 800W AC class. The additional panel capacity can help the system produce more useful energy across changing daylight conditions, but it does not turn the socket-side output into a 1,260W system.

Wp — panel capacity The combined rated DC capacity of the solar panels under standard test conditions.
W / VA — inverter output The AC output capability of the microinverter. This is the figure that matters when considering the electrical connection.

What about EcoFlow, Anker and other solar brands?

A well-known solar brand isn't automatically a compliant plug-in solar option.

Check the exact product, not just the brand

Solar brands such as EcoFlow and Anker offer a range of solar, battery and portable-energy products. However, products designed for other applications should not automatically be treated as compliant plug-in solar systems for the GB socket route.

The important question is whether the exact model and configuration you're considering meets the relevant requirements and has the appropriate current registration or certification.

This is why our comparisons focus on individual systems rather than assuming that every product sold by a particular manufacturer is suitable for plug-in solar.

Where can plug-in solar kits be installed?

The right mounting option depends on the product and your property. Don't assume a kit can be installed in every location.

Garden

Ground-mounted systems can be useful where there is suitable outdoor space and a safe way to position and secure the equipment.

Balcony

Some products are specifically designed for balcony mounting. Weight, fixing arrangements, landlord or building permissions and product suitability all need checking.

Wall

Some hybrid systems can be mounted to suitable structures. The manufacturer's mounting instructions should always be followed.

Flat roof / outbuilding

Certain systems can be suitable for flat roofs, sheds or outbuildings where the mounting method and structure are appropriate.

Check the exact model and current relevant registration or certification rather than relying on a brand name or retailer claim.

How much do plug-in solar kits cost?

Prices vary depending on panel capacity, mounting equipment, inverter, included accessories and retailer.

Don't compare price alone

A kit with a higher headline panel capacity may cost more, but that doesn't automatically mean it will produce proportionally more useful electricity at your property.

Panel orientation, shading, location, household electricity use and the amount of solar generation you can use directly all affect the value you get from a system.

Price per Wp can be useful for comparing headline panel capacity, but it isn't the same thing as the cost of the electricity you generate or your eventual payback period.

Plug-in solar rules, compliance & safety

Buying a kit is only one part of the decision. The exact product, connection method and installation circumstances matter.

Check the exact system before buying

The plug-in solar route introduced in Great Britain allows compliant plug-in solar systems to connect through a standard mains socket subject to the applicable requirements. The current route is associated with an 800W-class AC output limit and the relevant G98/ENA requirements.

Importantly, this does not mean that any solar panel, microinverter or portable power product can simply be plugged into a domestic socket. The exact product and configuration need to be suitable.

Product compliance is also different from deciding whether a particular location, socket, structure or property is suitable. Building, property, electrical and installation considerations may still apply.

The new plug-in solar route applies to Great Britain. Northern Ireland has separate electricity connection arrangements, so the information on this page should not be treated as confirmation that the GB route applies there.

If you're uncertain about a product or installation, check the manufacturer's instructions and seek appropriate professional advice before proceeding.

Plug-in solar kit FAQs

Some of the most common questions people ask before comparing plug-in solar systems.

What is a plug-in solar kit?

A plug-in solar kit is a small-scale solar system designed to generate electricity and connect to the home's electrical system through the applicable plug-in route. A typical kit includes solar panels, a microinverter, mounting equipment and cables.

Are plug-in solar kits legal in the UK?

Plug-in solar systems meeting the applicable requirements can now use the new route in Great Britain. The rules and network requirements apply to the exact system and configuration, so you should check the product rather than assuming every solar kit is suitable.

Why do some plug-in solar kits have more than 800W of panels?

The 800W-class figure relates to AC inverter output, while the panels are rated by their DC capacity in Wp. A kit can therefore have more than 800Wp of panels while using an inverter in the 800W AC class.

What is the difference between Wp and W?

Wp describes the rated peak DC capacity of solar panels under standard test conditions. W or VA on the inverter side describes the AC output capability of the inverter.

Can plug-in solar panels be used on a balcony?

Some systems are specifically designed or marketed for balcony use, but not every plug-in solar kit is suitable. Check the manufacturer's mounting instructions, weight, fixing method and any property or building requirements before installation.

Can plug-in solar panels be installed in a garden?

Some kits are specifically designed for ground or garden installation. Check the manufacturer's instructions for the permitted mounting arrangement, anchoring or ballast requirements and the suitability of the location.

How much do plug-in solar kits cost?

Current prices vary by product, panel capacity, mounting system, retailer and included equipment. Our comparison shows indicative prices where we have been able to verify them, but prices can change and should be checked before purchase.

Do I need to check the ENA register?

The exact product's current network compliance information is an important part of checking whether a plug-in system is suitable for the relevant GB connection route. Always check the exact model rather than relying only on the manufacturer's brand name.

Are EcoFlow and Anker plug-in solar systems compliant?

You should not assume that a particular EcoFlow, Anker or other branded solar product is compliant simply because the company sells solar equipment. Check the exact model and configuration against the applicable GB requirements before treating it as a plug-in solar system.

Can plug-in solar be used with a battery?

Battery storage is a separate consideration from the basic plug-in solar route. The relevant GB plug-in solar specification covers plug-in solar systems without batteries, so battery configurations need to be considered separately rather than assumed to fall under the same route.
