Plug-in Solar Installation Checklist: What You Need to Do in the UK

A practical 10-step checklist for Great Britain: what to check before buying, what to check before installing, how the current 800 VA framework works, and what to do after your plug-in solar device is in place.

Start the Checklist
Current framework from 27 August 2026 · Great Britain only · Informational guidance

What do you actually need to do before and after installing plug-in solar?

Plug-in solar is now permitted in Great Britain under a specific framework for qualifying domestic plug-in solar devices. The practical question is not simply whether a product is advertised as “plug and play” or “800W”.

You need to check that the exact device qualifies, understand its AC output and panel capacity, make sure it is suitable for your property, deal with any property permissions, follow the manufacturer's instructions and complete the current registration route.

This page is designed as a practical plug-in solar installation checklist for the UK, rather than a general explanation of the legal background. For the wider regulatory picture, see our UK Rules & Safety guide.

Important: Great Britain only

The new framework applies to England, Scotland and Wales. Do not assume that the same plug-in solar route automatically applies in Northern Ireland.

In Great Britain, the current product specification sets a maximum apparent power of 800 VA, with approximately 3.5 A AC output. The specification also allows PV modules with a combined DC rating up to 2,000W behind the compliant AC output limit.

These are different measurements. The panel DC rating is not the same thing as the inverter/device AC output.

The 10-step plug-in solar installation checklist

Work through these checks before you buy, install or switch on a qualifying plug-in solar device in Great Britain.

1

Check that plug-in solar is suitable for your property

Start with the property and the proposed location. A technically compliant product can still be unsuitable for a particular home or mounting position.

  • How much direct sunlight reaches the proposed location?
  • Is there significant shade from trees, buildings or other obstacles?
  • Which direction does the panel location face?
  • Is there a safe and suitable place for the panels and mounting equipment?
  • Are you in a house, flat or another property type?
  • If using a balcony, is the balcony structure and mounting arrangement suitable?
  • If you rent, have you checked the permissions that apply to your tenancy?
Remember: property permission is separate from electrical and network compliance. A product can meet the plug-in solar specification while your property still has separate permission requirements.
2

Choose a compliant plug-in solar product

Do not treat “plug and play”, “balcony solar” or “800W” as proof that a kit qualifies under the current Great Britain framework.

  • Check the exact model on the ENA Type Test Register before buying.
  • Check the manufacturer's technical documentation.
  • Check that the device is eligible under the current interim product specification.
  • Check the relevant Energy Networks Association product/type-test information.
  • Make sure you are looking at a solar-only plug-in device where the framework requires it.
Key point: being sold in the UK, having a UK plug or carrying “800W” in the product name does not by itself prove compliance.
3

Check the inverter/device output

The current domestic plug-in route uses an 800 VA maximum apparent power limit for the qualifying device. This is about the AC side of the system.

  • 800 VA: the current maximum apparent power for the qualifying plug-in device.
  • Approximately 3.5 A: the corresponding AC output figure in the specification.
  • Up to 2,000W DC PV: the specification allows a larger combined panel rating behind the AC limit where the product complies with the specification.

This means an advert mentioning, for example, 800W of panels is not necessarily describing an 800W AC plug-in system. Panel DC capacity and inverter/device AC output are separate specifications.

4

Check exactly what is included in the kit

Before buying, make a simple inventory of what you are actually getting and whether the manufacturer's documentation covers the complete arrangement.

  • Solar panels
  • Inverter or plug-in solar device
  • Cables and connectors
  • Plug and connection equipment
  • Mounting equipment
  • Monitoring equipment or app, if supplied
  • Installation and safety instructions
  • Compliance and product documentation

Comparing the complete kit matters because a low headline price may not include the mounting, monitoring or other equipment you need for the intended location.

5

Check the electrical connection and follow manufacturer instructions

The current route is intended for qualifying plug-in solar devices designed for the specified domestic connection arrangement. Follow the manufacturer's instructions exactly.

  • Read the complete installation and safety instructions before use.
  • Use the supplied or specified connection equipment.
  • Do not improvise with cables, adapters or connection arrangements.
  • Do not carry out electrical work beyond the specified plug-in arrangement yourself.
  • Where electrical work is required outside the plug-in arrangement, use a suitably qualified professional.
Safety first: this page does not provide step-by-step electrical wiring instructions. If you are unsure whether your home's electrical installation is suitable, get appropriate professional advice.
6

Check property permissions

Electrical/network compliance is only one part of the decision. You may also need permission relating to the property or the proposed mounting location.

  • Landlord permission for rented properties
  • Freeholder permission for leasehold properties
  • Lease restrictions or conditions
  • Building management requirements for flats
  • Planning considerations where relevant
  • Listed-building or other property-specific requirements where relevant
  • Balcony mounting restrictions or structural considerations

If you rent or lease your property, check the relevant documents and get any required permission before buying or installing equipment.

7

Make sure you're using the correct type of system

The simplified plug-in solar framework does not automatically cover every small solar or battery arrangement.

  • Conventional hard-wired rooftop PV can have different connection requirements.
  • Other electricity-generation systems can have different requirements.
  • Plug-in battery systems are outside the current plug-in solar product specification.
  • Plug-in solar devices integrated with battery systems are also outside that specification.

If your proposed system is not a qualifying plug-in solar device, do not assume that this checklist or the simplified route applies. See our UK Rules & Safety page for the wider picture.

8

Remember the one-device household limit

Under the current Great Britain connection framework, the present position is: The current G98 connection rules limit plug-in solar to one device per household.

Do not assume that buying two 800W devices is permitted simply because each device is individually rated within the 800 VA limit. The current framework should be checked before connecting any additional device.

9

Register the compliant plug-in solar device

For qualifying plug-in solar in Great Britain, the current consumer registration route is myplugin.solar. The service is intended to notify the relevant electricity network operator.

  • Check the exact device/model before registering.
  • Use the current registration service for the qualifying plug-in route.
  • Keep your registration confirmation with your other system records.
  • Do not assume that every solar installation uses the same registration process.
Important: conventional hard-wired solar and other generation arrangements can have different DNO notification requirements. The simplified consumer route described here is for qualifying plug-in solar devices.
10

Keep your records and monitor the system

Once the system is in use, keep a simple record of what you bought and what was registered.

  • Purchase receipt
  • Exact make and model
  • Manufacturer instructions and documentation
  • Compliance information
  • Registration confirmation
  • Monitoring information and generation data, if available
  • Any relevant property permission

Continue to monitor the equipment and check for visible damage, faults or other warning signs. Follow the manufacturer's instructions if something appears wrong.

What should you compare when choosing a plug-in solar kit?

Use the whole system, not just the headline wattage or price, when comparing kits.

What to compare What to check
Compliance Whether the exact device/model meets the current Great Britain plug-in solar requirements and relevant manufacturer documentation.
Inverter/device AC output/apparent power, product specification and whether the device is the qualifying plug-in type.
Panel capacity Combined DC panel rating and how it relates to the device's AC output. Do not compare these figures as though they are the same measurement.
Panel dimensions Physical size, weight and whether the panels fit the intended location safely.
Mounting What is included, where it can be used and whether your property permits the proposed mounting arrangement.
Monitoring Whether the kit provides generation monitoring and whether the information is easy for you to understand.
Warranty Panel, inverter/device and other component warranty periods and what the warranty actually covers.
Total price The complete cost of the equipment and any additional mounting or professional work that may be required.
Property suitability Sunlight, orientation, shade, space, mounting location, property type and any landlord/freeholder/building requirements.

Which type of plug-in solar kit might suit your situation?

Different households may have different priorities. These are practical questions to use when comparing options, not product rankings.

Lower upfront cost

Compare the total kit price and what is included rather than focusing only on the advertised panel or inverter figure.

Limited space

Check panel dimensions, mounting requirements, available sunlight and whether the intended balcony, terrace or other location is suitable.

Monitoring

If understanding generation matters to you, compare how the kit reports output and whether the monitoring information is clear and accessible.

Understanding generation

Consider whether the system gives you useful information about generation so you can understand when your panels are producing electricity.

Future energy equipment

If you are considering batteries or other energy equipment later, check that you understand which products are outside the current plug-in solar framework.

Flats or rented properties

Property permissions can be just as important as technical specifications. Check landlord, freeholder, lease and building requirements before buying.

How much electricity could your plug-in solar actually save?

The amount of electricity generated by plug-in solar varies from property to property. Location, panel orientation, shading, season and the available sunlight all affect generation.

Your potential bill saving also depends on how much electricity your household uses and when it uses it. Using more electricity while the solar system is generating can affect how much of the generation is used directly in the home. This is often described as self-consumption.

Frequently asked questions

Practical answers to common plug-in solar installation questions in 2026.

Is plug-in solar legal in the UK in 2026?

The new framework applies in Great Britain — England, Scotland and Wales — from 27 August 2026 for qualifying plug-in solar devices that meet the current requirements. Do not assume the same framework automatically applies in Northern Ireland.

What is the 800W plug-in solar limit?

The current product specification uses a maximum apparent power of 800 VA for the qualifying plug-in solar device, corresponding to approximately 3.5 A AC output. This should not be confused with the DC rating of the solar panels. The specification allows PV modules with a combined DC rating up to 2,000W behind the compliant 800 VA AC output limit.

Do I need to register plug-in solar?

For qualifying plug-in solar in Great Britain, the current consumer route is myplugin.solar. It is intended to notify the relevant network operator. Keep confirmation of the registration with your system records.

Can I install two 800W plug-in solar systems?

The current connection framework limits this route to one plug-in solar device per household. Do not assume that two individually compliant 800 VA devices can be connected at the same property.

Do I need an electrician?

The qualifying plug-in arrangement is designed for connection using the specified plug-in setup, but this does not mean every electrical task around a solar project is suitable for DIY work. Follow the manufacturer's instructions and use a suitably qualified professional where electrical work outside the specified plug-in arrangement is required.

Can I use plug-in solar in a flat or rented property?

Potentially, but property permissions are a separate issue from electrical and network compliance. A flat or rented property may involve landlord, freeholder, lease, building-management, planning or mounting requirements. Check these before buying or installing equipment.

Is an 800W solar panel the same as an 800W plug-in solar system?

No. A panel's wattage is a DC rating. The plug-in device has its own AC output or apparent-power rating. The current qualifying route has an 800 VA device limit, while the product specification permits a combined PV module DC rating up to 2,000W.

What is myplugin.solar?

It is the current consumer registration route for qualifying plug-in solar in Great Britain. The service is intended to notify the relevant electricity network operator of the qualifying device.

Do I need to tell my DNO about plug-in solar?

The current framework includes network notification for qualifying plug-in solar, with the consumer registration route at myplugin.solar. Do not apply the same notification process automatically to conventional hard-wired solar, batteries or other generation systems, which can have different requirements.
