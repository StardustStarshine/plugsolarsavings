Plug-in solar can be a simple way for some households to generate electricity at home, but the potential benefit depends on how and where the system is used. Plug Solar Savings helps you understand the basics and estimate what plug-in solar could potentially save you.

What is plug-in solar?

Plug-in solar is a small-scale solar setup designed to generate electricity for use in a home or other suitable property. Systems can use solar panels with an inverter and other components, with the exact setup, connection method and requirements depending on the system and circumstances.

In Great Britain, there are specific requirements and safety considerations to understand before using a plug-in solar system. Our UK Rules & Safety guide explains the main points to check.

What can plug-in solar help power?

Plug-in solar can contribute electricity to the everyday appliances and devices in your home while your system is generating. How much it can contribute depends on the system, your electricity use and when appliances are running.

TV

Use solar-generated electricity to help power your TV and other entertainment devices during the day.

Fridge & Freezer

Your fridge and freezer use electricity throughout the day, making them a natural part of household daytime demand.

Wi-Fi & Routers

Keep everyday connected devices running while your solar system is producing electricity.

Laptop & Devices

Charging laptops, tablets and other devices during daylight can help you make direct use of solar generation.

Lighting

Solar electricity can contribute towards your home's wider electricity demand, including lighting and smaller appliances.

Home Appliances

Washing machines, dishwashers and other appliances can benefit when their electricity use lines up with solar generation.

Is plug-in solar right for you?

Plug-in solar isn't automatically right for every home. Your potential benefit depends on factors such as where the panels can be positioned, available sunlight, shading, your electricity use and how much of the electricity generated you can use.

Your daytime electricity use

The more electricity you use while your solar system is generating, the more opportunity you may have to use that electricity directly.

Your available sunlight

Orientation, shading, position and location all affect how much electricity a solar system can generate.

Your property

Flats, houses and other properties can have different practical considerations, including positioning and permissions.

Check before you buy

Plug-in solar is not simply a case of buying a panel and connecting it anywhere. Product specifications, electrical requirements, positioning, building or property considerations and applicable UK rules should all be checked before installation or use.
