Solar Panels & Components

Solar panels are only one part of a complete solar PV system. Understand the main solar panel components, what each one does and what UK homeowners should compare when looking at solar equipment.

What components are needed for a solar panel system?

A complete solar PV system is made up of several connected parts. The solar panels generate electricity from sunlight, while the inverter, mounting equipment, cables, electrical protection and monitoring equipment all have important jobs to do.

The exact solar panel system components depend on the type and size of installation, the property, the roof or ground location and the system design. Comparing the panel alone can therefore give an incomplete picture of the equipment you are buying.

Main solar power components

  • Solar panels
  • Solar inverter
  • Microinverters where applicable
  • Solar panel mounting system
  • Solar cables and connectors
  • Electrical protection and isolation equipment
  • Monitoring equipment

Solar panels

Solar PV panels are the part of the system that captures sunlight and converts it into DC electricity.

W

Panel wattage and Wp

Panel output is commonly stated in watts peak (Wp). Looking at Wp helps compare the rated capacity of individual solar panels, but useful system output also depends on sunlight, orientation, shading and other conditions.

%

Solar panel efficiency

Efficiency describes how effectively a panel converts available sunlight into electricity. Higher efficiency can be useful where roof space is limited, but it is only one factor to compare.

+

Panel type and design

Monocrystalline panels are widely used in residential solar. When comparing solar panels UK homeowners should also look at dimensions, rated output, warranties, product documentation and how the panel fits the available installation area.

How many solar panels do I need?

There is no single panel count that suits every home. The number of solar panels you may need depends on your electricity use, desired system size, the output of the chosen panels, available roof or ground space, orientation and shading.

A larger panel with a higher Wp rating can provide more rated capacity per panel, while a higher-efficiency panel can make better use of limited roof space. A system designer should consider the property and the complete system rather than choosing a panel count in isolation.

Solar inverters & microinverters

The inverter is a key part of the system because your home normally uses AC electricity while solar panels generate DC electricity.

DC

What does a solar inverter do?

A solar inverter converts the DC electricity produced by solar panels into AC electricity that can be used by compatible household electrical equipment and connected to the property's electrical system as part of a correctly designed installation.

S

String or central inverter

A string inverter is commonly used with multiple panels connected into one or more strings. System design needs to account for the number of panels, electrical characteristics, roof arrangement and shading conditions.

M

Microinverters

A solar microinverter is installed at panel level rather than using one central inverter for the whole array. Microinverter systems can be useful for some designs where panel-level conversion suits the installation.

There is no universal answer that a string inverter or microinverter is always better. The right solar panel inverter depends on the system design, property and installation requirements.

Solar panel mounting systems

The mounting system holds the panels in the required position and must be suitable for the roof, ground or other installation location.

R

Roof mounting

Roof-mounted solar uses a mounting arrangement designed for the particular roof type and installation. The system needs to provide secure support while working with the roof structure and panel layout.

B

Solar panel brackets

Brackets and fixings connect the mounting structure to the installation surface. The appropriate hardware depends on the roof construction, mounting system and structural requirements.

—

Solar panel rails

Rails provide a mounting track to position and secure panels. Rail size, fixing method and compatibility should match the mounting system and installation requirements.

Other mounting arrangements

Flat-roof systems may use mounting frames designed for the roof surface and may have different structural and loading considerations. Ground-mounted solar uses its own support structure and foundations or fixing arrangements. The mounting system should always be selected for the actual installation location rather than treated as a universal accessory.

Cables, connectors & electrical components

Cables and electrical equipment connect the system together and help it operate as a coordinated installation.

C

Solar cables

Solar cables are selected for the electrical characteristics and environment of the installation. Cable type, rating, routing and compatibility need to be considered as part of the system.

↔

Solar connectors

Connectors join compatible parts of a solar installation. MC4-type connectors are commonly seen on PV equipment, but connectors should not be assumed to be interchangeable simply because they look similar.

!

Electrical protection

A complete installation may include electrical protection and isolation equipment appropriate to the system. These components should be specified and installed as part of the overall electrical design.

Compatibility matters

Solar panel accessories are not automatically compatible with every panel, inverter or mounting system. Check manufacturer documentation, connector compatibility, electrical ratings and the requirements of the complete installation.

This page is intended to help homeowners compare solar panel installation components at a high level. It does not provide DIY electrical wiring instructions.

Solar panel monitoring

Monitoring can make it easier to understand how a solar PV system is performing after installation.

Generation information

Monitoring can show how much electricity the system is generating, helping homeowners see patterns over time and understand changing sunlight conditions.

Inverter display or app

Depending on the equipment, information may be available through an inverter display, mobile app or online monitoring platform.

What to compare

When comparing systems, check what monitoring is included, what information it provides, whether there are ongoing charges and how long the monitoring service is expected to remain available.

How solar panel components work together

The main energy path can be explained simply as: Sun → Solar Panels → Inverter → Home.

Basic solar electricity flow

Sun

Sunlight provides the energy that the PV panels capture.

→

→

Inverter

The inverter converts DC electricity into AC electricity.

→

Home

Electricity can be used by compatible household loads as part of the property's electrical system.

Holds the panels in the required position and provides the physical support for the installation.

Cables & connectors

Provide compatible electrical connections between system components.

I

Protection & monitoring

Electrical protection and monitoring equipment support managed operation and visibility of system performance.

Choosing solar components: a homeowner checklist

Comparing the complete solar panel equipment package can be more useful than comparing one component or headline price in isolation.

Panel output

Compare the rated Wp output of the panels and the proposed overall system size in kWp.

Efficiency

Consider solar panel efficiency alongside panel dimensions and available roof or ground space.

Compatibility

Check that panels, inverter, mounting equipment, cables, connectors and monitoring equipment are designed to work together.

Mounting requirements

Make sure the proposed mounting system suits the roof, flat roof, ground location or other installation surface.

Warranties

Check product warranties, performance warranties where applicable, exclusions and who provides the warranty support.

Monitoring

Check whether generation monitoring is included and what information you will be able to see after installation.

Complete system price

Compare what is actually included, including mounting, electrical equipment, cables, connectors, monitoring and installation where relevant.

Installer experience

Consider the installer's experience with the proposed equipment, property type and system design.

Documentation

Ask for clear model numbers, specifications, warranty information, installation documentation and other relevant system records.

Professional installation & UK safety

Solar panel installation involves electrical, structural and property considerations, particularly for rooftop systems.

Use suitably qualified installers

Homeowners should use suitably qualified and experienced professionals for complete solar PV installations. The appropriate installation arrangements depend on the property, equipment, electrical system and applicable requirements.

MCS is an established certification scheme used within the UK renewable-energy sector. Where MCS certification is relevant to the installation, ask the installer about their current certification and what it covers. MCS status should not be treated as a statement that every individual component purchase requires MCS certification.

Before buying solar panel equipment, check the exact product specifications, compatibility, installation requirements, warranties and relevant property or electrical considerations. Manufacturer instructions and professional advice should be followed where needed.

This page is general homeowner information and is not a substitute for a site survey, electrical design or professional installation advice.

Solar panel components FAQs

Common questions about solar panels, inverters, mounting systems and other solar panel system components.

What components are needed for a solar panel system?

A typical solar PV system can include solar panels, an inverter, mounting equipment, solar cables and compatible connectors, electrical protection and isolation equipment, and monitoring. The exact solar panel installation components depend on the system design and property.

What does a solar inverter do?

A solar inverter converts the DC electricity generated by solar panels into AC electricity used by compatible household electrical equipment and the property's electrical system.

What is a microinverter?

A microinverter is a type of solar inverter installed at panel level rather than using one central inverter for the whole array. A solar microinverter can form part of a system where panel-level conversion suits the design.

What are solar panel mounting systems?

Solar panel mounting systems are the brackets, rails, fixings and support structures used to position and secure solar panels. The appropriate system depends on whether the panels are being installed on a pitched roof, flat roof, ground or another suitable location.

What should I look for when buying solar panels?

Compare panel output in Wp, efficiency, dimensions, warranties, product documentation and compatibility with the proposed inverter and mounting system. Also consider available installation space, orientation and shading rather than judging a panel on headline wattage alone.

How many solar panels does a home need?

The number varies by household electricity use, desired system size, panel output, available roof or ground space, orientation and shading. A suitable panel count should be considered as part of the overall system design.

Are solar panel components interchangeable?

Not necessarily. Panels, inverters, mounting systems, cables and connectors have specific electrical, mechanical and installation requirements. Always check manufacturer documentation and compatibility for the exact models.

Do solar panels need professional installation?

Complete rooftop solar installations involve electrical and structural considerations, so homeowners should use suitably qualified and experienced installers. The appropriate installation arrangements depend on the system and property.

Can I add a battery to solar panels later?

A battery may be added to some solar systems later, but compatibility depends on the existing panels, inverter, electrical design and proposed battery system. For more information, see the separate Batteries page.

What is the difference between solar panels and solar components?

Solar panels are the part of a PV system that converts sunlight into DC electricity. Solar components is the wider term covering the panels plus the inverter, mounting equipment, cables, connectors, electrical protection, monitoring and other equipment required by the particular system.
